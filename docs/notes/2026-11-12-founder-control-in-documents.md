# Founder control in company documents (2026-11-12, founder call)

Oyin's standing rule: every company document must keep him in control of Urganize until he chooses to step away. Nobody (investors, the board, new shareholders, a buyer) should be able to remove him, outvote him or block him without his yes. Prompted by a post on how Zuckerberg's control lets Meta make long, unpopular bets (`worklog/pastes/2026-11-12-zuck-control-meta.md`).

## When this applies

Any time a session reviews or drafts: shareholder agreements, articles of association, term sheets, SAFEs or convertible notes, investment or subscription agreements, board resolutions, share option or ESOP plans, co-founder or employment agreements for senior hires, partnership or distribution deals that grant equity or control, and any sale or merger paper.

## What to check, every time

1. **Votes.** Does Oyin keep a majority of voting power after this document? Model it after the deal, not before. Look for share classes with extra votes for founder shares (check what Nigerian company law allows with a lawyer).
2. **Board.** Can he appoint most of the directors? Is he chair? Does a board meeting need him present to count (quorum)?
3. **Removing the CEO.** Can anyone remove him as CEO or director without his consent? Any "for cause" removal must be narrow and clearly defined.
4. **Vetoes.** List every investor consent right (reserved matters). Flag any over hiring or firing, budget, product, strategy, new fundraising or the CEO. Keep them to true investor protection only.
5. **Leaving and vesting.** Founder vesting, good and bad leaver terms: can they be used to take his shares back or push him out?
6. **Drag-along and sale.** Can others force a sale of the company over his no? What threshold, and does it need his vote?
7. **Dilution.** Anti-dilution, option pool size and future rounds: how far could his voting share fall, and does he have a say?
8. **Votes signed away.** Proxies, voting agreements or powers of attorney that hand his votes to someone else.
9. **Share transfers.** Right of first refusal and transfer limits on others, so no stranger buys a controlling stake.
10. **Stepping away on his terms.** He leaves when he decides, and can keep founder share votes or a board seat after stepping back if he wants.

## How to report

Open the review with a yes or no: "Does this keep you in control?" Then list each clause that weakens control, quoted with its clause number, what it lets someone do to him in plain words, and a suggested fix. Label what was checked against the text versus assumed.

This is a checklist, not legal advice. Anything binding goes past a Nigerian corporate lawyer before he signs, and nothing is signed or sent without his yes.
