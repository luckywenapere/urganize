The brief, as a board

Opening a rollout now lands here. Top half is the vision: the cover, the idea, how it should feel, what it looks and sounds like. Bottom half is the work: key moments, what we are making, who is on what. Cards sit on a grid; the team adds, leads arrange. Sample data is the real "Honest" rollout. Photos are stand-ins.

1

Brief opens first. It moves to the first tab. Present and Share sit where every eye ends up after reading the tabs.

2

Vision on top, work below. A creative reads top down and stops at Sound. Ops skims straight to the moments line and the list.

3

One number everyone feels. Days to release, big. Progress and what is late sit under it, so nobody has to open the plan to know if we are fine.

4

A card you can grab. Select a card to move or resize it. It snaps to the grid, so the board never looks messy.

5

Notes from the team. A sticky note is the fastest way to say "what if". Anyone on the rollout can drop one.

6

What we are making. Each deliverable is a plan task: owner, due date and status come from the plan, so there is one place to update.

Staff review · what each lens changed

Four passes over this mock. Each line is something that changed because of the review, or a call left for you.

DSStaff designer

  • Hierarchy by size, not boxes. The cover takes 5 of 12 columns so the eye lands on the art, then the idea, then the countdown. No type card or budget card; twelve equal cards would read as a dashboard.
  • Fixed on the first render: palette names were cut off at this width, the card toolbar covered the title on the cover, the sticky hid an image, and Release and Week 1 recap collided on the moments line.
  • Two type voices only. Young Serif for the feeling (title, idea, mood, countdown), Instrument Sans for facts. Mono only for dates, so dates line up and scan.
  • Colour comes from the record. The app stays neutral cream so the cover and palette carry the mood. Brand green is kept for done and release day only.
  • Captions sit on the images. That is how art directors annotate a board: the note lives where you look.

PMStaff PM

  • Ops can answer "are we fine?" in 3 seconds: days to go, 2 late, today's line on the moments track. Nothing on the board asks them to open the plan first.
  • Deliverables are plan tasks, not a second list. Mark a task as a piece and it shows here. One place to update, no drift.
  • "Seen the brief: 3 of 3" tells the lead the team has actually read it before the meeting.
  • Moves the scoreboard: guests join from the link to tick their part, and those joins are what make a team "2 or more active". Measure: link opens, guest joins, time on Brief vs Plan.

ENStaff engineer

  • One new table, release_brief_cards: kind, x, y, w, h, payload, author. Idea, references and decisions keep their tables and render as cards, so nothing is migrated away.
  • 12-column grid, whole cells only. Drag and resize with pointer events, snap on drop, save one row per move. Phone order is y then x, no second layout to keep.
  • Live cards are read, not stored. Countdown, moments, pieces, who's on what come from tasks on every load, as Dates and Who's on what do today.
  • Images: a brief-images bucket, 10 MB cap, resized on upload; palette is read from the cover in the browser and saved as five hex values.
  • Last write wins per card, not per page, so two people editing different cards never clash. Live cursors are not in V1.

VPStaff VP

  • This is the screen we demo to a label. It should look like what their creative director already makes in Keynote, but alive and tied to the work.
  • The shared link is the growth loop. Every outside creative who gets a brief sees Urganize at its best, then joins to tick their part.
  • Scope held: no free drawing, no infinite canvas, no audio uploads, no budget card. Each can come later if real teams ask.
  • Risk: a board with no images looks empty. UrgAns's first board fills every slot it can, and empty slots say what to drop in.

Changes this makes to what exists today

  • Brief becomes the first tab and the page a rollout opens on. Work the plan moves to second.
  • The shared link adds the guest's own pieces and a Join button, and follows the phone's light or dark setting instead of always dark.
  • The approve and lock step stays: once approved, the team can still add notes, but only the owner or a team admin can move, resize or delete cards.
