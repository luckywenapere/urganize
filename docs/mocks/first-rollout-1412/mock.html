A new person should see their plan in the first minute

People make an account and leave before they add any music. Below is what we measured, what a new person meets today, and three ways to fix it. The screens are drawn for a phone, because 13 of the 16 real accounts since 3 Nov signed up on one. Nothing here is built. Names and dates on the new screens are made up.

Why nobody adds music
  1. 1
    Email sign-up is turned away. Type a name, an email and a password and the app says Urganize is invite-only. Only Google gets in. This is a bug and it is live now.
  2. 2
    Two screens of questions come before any plan. 3 people stopped at the team screen. 5 more made a team, then stopped at the project screen or the plan wall.
  3. 3
    The answer picked for you gives an empty page. "No date yet" is selected when the form opens. It leads to a page that asks for "the idea" and shows no plan. All 3 rollouts made this month have no date.
  4. 4
    The price comes before the plan. Five prices sit under the sign-in page, and a priced trial sheet covers the first screen inside the app.
  5. 5
    Nobody is asked to come back. The 8 who stopped got no email asking them to finish. The welcome emails only start after sign-up is finished.

What a new person meets on a phone

Real screens from the local test app, 27 Nov, in the app's dark theme.

Option ARecommended

One screen about their song, then the plan

After the account there is one screen. It asks about the song, makes the team quietly and opens a full plan. The team screen and "Nothing here yet." both go.

What changes
  • The team screen goes. The team is made as "Tunde's Team" and can be renamed in Team.
  • Five stages become three. "Making it" still saves a project and opens the project page, as it does today.
  • "It's me" tells us they are the artist. Anyone else is treated as a manager.
  • "Not sure" puts the release 8 weeks out, so nobody lands on an empty page. This is a new rule and needs your yes.
Why it should work
Phone visits are short. Most first visits here were under 5 minutes. One screen about their own song is easier to finish than two screens about setting up. They see the plan while they still care, and the price comes after they have seen something worth paying for. This is a prediction. We have no click data.
What it costs
Medium. Most of the work is the sign-up page and its loading screen. Teams, rollouts and plans are made the same way they are today.
How we will know
New accounts that have a rollout with a date within 10 minutes of signing up. Today that is 0 of 16.
Option B

Straight in, with an example plan

No questions after the account. The app opens on a ready example, and one tap puts their own song on it.

What changes
  • The team screen and the project screen both go.
  • Every new account starts with an example rollout, marked as a test so it stays out of our numbers.
  • "Use my song" turns the example into their real rollout.
Why it could work
They see the product before they type anything. A new person who is not sure what Urganize does gets the answer on the first screen.
The risk
Some will look at the example, feel they have seen it, and leave without adding their own song. We would still have no real rollout from them, and it gets harder to tell who was serious.
What it costs
The most work. Turning an example into a real rollout is new, and every number we keep has to leave examples out.
Option C

Keep the two setup screens, make each one shorter

The flow you approved on 29 Oct stays. Each screen gets shorter, and the answer picked for you leads to a plan.

What changes
  • Team: three cards, one tap.
  • Project: the example card and the two extra cards (bring in released music, invite your team) move to Home.
  • "No date yet" and "Skip for now" stay for people who want them.
Why it could work
It is the smallest change to a flow people already pass through, and each screen is quicker to finish.
The risk
There are still two screens between the account and the plan. 8 of the 16 left across those two screens.
What it costs
The least work.
